\begin{abstract}
Data circulation across mutually distrustful organizations requires more than file-level integrity. A practical audit system must support traceable authorization, verifiable off-chain evidence, accountable recovery, and low-cost confirmation under frequent audit events. BLoP provides a full-node trusted audit model that combines blockchain, trusted computing, privacy-preserving computation, anomaly monitoring, and PBFT-style confirmation. This paper studies a lightweight extension of that model, called TrustAuditFlow, for scenarios where audit events are frequent and not all events require full-node confirmation. TrustAuditFlow keeps BLoP as the strong-security baseline, but adds a risk-aware committee path: low- and medium-risk audit events are aggregated off-chain, committed through Merkle roots and hash-chain anchors, and confirmed by a trusted audit committee; high-risk or disputed events fall back to the BLoP-style full-node path. The proposed design further connects data sharding, sampled integrity checking, erasure-code-based recovery, on-chain accountability records, and trust-score updates into a closed audit-recovery loop, providing a structured workflow for blockchain-based distributed data verification in data circulation.
\end{abstract}
