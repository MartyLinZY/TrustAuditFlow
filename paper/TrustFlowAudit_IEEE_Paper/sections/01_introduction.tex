\section{Introduction}

Data circulation refers to controlled data use across organizations, platforms, and computing environments. In such settings, trust is not limited to whether a stored file remains unchanged. A deployable system must also answer whether the data use was authorized, whether the computation process can be audited, whether the evidence is tamper-evident, and whether responsibility can be assigned after an incident. Financial, medical, government, and industrial collaboration scenarios often avoid direct plaintext data transfer. Instead, they rely on privacy-preserving computation, trusted execution, data connectors, or controlled APIs. As a result, the target of verification expands from static files to data shards, computation logs, model updates, task digests, result hashes, and recovery events.

Classical cloud-storage auditing mainly answers whether remote data are still correctly stored. Provable Data Possession (PDP) reduces verification cost through challenge-response protocols and homomorphic tags~\cite{ateniese_provable_2007}. Proofs of Retrievability (PoR) strengthen this goal by requiring that the data remain recoverable after limited corruption~\cite{juels_pors_2007,shacham_compact_2013}. Dynamic PDP further supports authenticated updates, insertions, and deletions~\cite{erway_dynamic_2015}. These techniques provide the cryptographic foundation for remote integrity checking, but the audit process is often initiated by a user or a third-party auditor. In a multi-party data circulation workflow, the auditor itself may delay, omit, or misreport an audit, and the audit trail must be independently verifiable.

Blockchain-based auditing shifts the focus from ``can the data be checked'' to ``can the audit process itself be trusted.'' Prior work uses block hashes or nonces as unpredictable challenge sources, smart contracts for audit logging and penalties, and consortium-chain consensus to constrain procrastinating or malicious auditors~\cite{xue_identity-based_2019,zhang_blockchain-based_2021,fan_dredas_2020,lin_consortium_2022}. In the same direction, BLoP integrates blockchain, trusted computing, and privacy-preserving computation. It maintains trusted nodes through attestation and monitoring components, detects abnormal events, and uses PBFT-style confirmation instead of proof-of-work for consortium scenarios. This full-node confirmation model is valuable when strong consistency and high assurance dominate the cost concern.

The problem studied in this paper is narrower: given BLoP as the full-node trusted audit baseline, can we add a lightweight path for frequent, low- or medium-risk audit events without weakening the ability to fall back to full-node confirmation? This question matters because real data circulation workflows may generate a large number of routine audit records. If every record is confirmed by all trusted nodes and stored individually on chain, the system may spend most resources on routine evidence rather than high-risk incidents. TrustAuditFlow addresses this issue by separating the audit workflow into two paths. Routine events are batched, committed through Merkle roots and hash-chain anchors, and confirmed by a trusted committee selected from the BLoP-style trusted node set. Critical or disputed events fall back to the full-node path.

The key research challenge is not merely reducing blockchain writes. The design must preserve a verifiable chain of evidence, avoid making the committee a new single point of trust, connect integrity failures with recovery, and keep the claims inside the evidence supported by the current prototype. TrustAuditFlow therefore treats the blockchain as a timeline and accountability layer, not as a place for heavy proof computation or bulk evidence storage. Data shards, audit proofs, and recovery artifacts remain off chain; on chain, the system records commitments, summaries, recovery states, and trust-score changes.

This paper makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate a lightweight extension of the BLoP full-node audit model for high-frequency audit scenarios, while preserving full-node confirmation as the high-assurance fallback.
  \item We define a closed audit-recovery loop for data circulation: shard storage, sampled checking, anomaly detection, erasure-code-assisted recovery, on-chain anchoring, and trust-score update.
  \item We design a trust-score-driven audit committee mechanism that reduces routine confirmation cost without treating the committee as a replacement for the full trusted node set.
  \item We specify a layered architecture in which expensive verification and evidence storage remain off chain, while Merkle roots, hash-chain anchors, recovery states, and accountability records are committed on chain.
  \item We explicitly state the current evidence boundary: this version provides the problem formulation and protocol design, while empirical claims are deferred until the evaluation is completed.
\end{itemize}
